JF2: the join-free fall detector after the error analysis

JF2 is JF with seven changes taken from the JF error analysis. It still reads only the radar signal: no joins, no source recordings and no labelling rule. On the subject-held-out CV it scores .861 pooled on both seeds, against .752 for JF, and .788 and .778 for RS and Amir's chain, which both use joins. On the official val streams it scores .802 / .816 (seeds 0 / 1), against .702 for JF, and .839 when trained on 8 stitchings of the official training clips. That is still below RS (.857) and Amir's chain (.938).

  • It finds more falls and raises fewer false alarms. Pooled, it misses 69 / 54 falls against 142 for JF, and has 57 / 52 false positives against 90. F1 at tIoU .5 rises from .690 to .780 / .766.
  • Fold D, the official split, is the only fold where the join-based models still win. JF2 scores .810 on both seeds, against .852 for RS and .922 for Amir. On the official val streams alone the gap is the same (.802 / .816 vs .857 / .938).

1. The architecture

JF2 keeps JF's structure. A trunk turns every 32 s window into 400 tokens. Stage 1 asks 3 learned queries per window whether a fall is there and where it starts and ends. The stream stage then gathers every proposal of the same fall from all the windows that saw it, reads the stream around it, and decides once. The orange badges and text mark what JF2 changed.

What happens at inference, step by step

  1. Read the stream as 19 channels

    Per radar, the raw complex CIR (I, Q, |z|) and the same three after subtracting an 8 s centred moving mean. The second set removes the room's static scene and keeps what moves. A valid mask marks padding.

  2. Encode 32 s windows every 6 s

    Each window becomes 400 tokens (one per 0.08 s). Each place in the stream is seen by 4–5 windows, at different positions inside them.

  3. Propose falls per window

    3 queries per window each give P(fall) and start / end distributions at 20 ms. Every query with P > .05 becomes a proposal, with its hidden state and its distance to the window's edge.

  4. Group the proposals of the stream

    Strongest first: a proposal joins a group if it overlaps the group's leader at tIoU ≥ .2 JF or comes within 3 s of the group's extent JF2. The group's reference span is the P-weighted median of its members, and its region is the members ± 5 s (at most 24 s).

  5. Describe each region

    The stream tokens (the windows' tokens averaged, centre-weighted), the event physics (how the scene before the span differs from the scene after it, per radar, with the stillest stretches as anchors), and one token per member proposal.

  6. Refine and decode

    2 slots attend to all of it and each says P(fall) and a span. Slots with P > .5 become events, with the span chosen by MBR for mean F1 at tIoU .1/.3/.5. Duplicates (tIoU ≥ .5, or centres within 3 s of a stronger event) are dropped.

2. JF → JF2: what changed and why

The JF error analysis traced its errors to three causes: unseen recording sessions, falls shortly after a join, and misplaced spans. Each change targets one of them. All seven went into one run, so the gain below belongs to the whole set.

3. One stream, step by step

Official val stream val_01, 1393–1462 s, holds four falls in 69 s. Both models below are the fold D models of seed 0 (fold D holds out the official val subjects). JF2 finds all four. JF finds two, misplaces one, misses one and raises one false alarm. Hover any bar for its values.

  • F1 · Chute01 at 1401.9 sFive windows propose the same span with P ≈ 1.000, and they form one group in both models. The JF2 slot says P 1.000, 1402.00–1404.46 s: tIoU 0.89 (JF 0.87). An easy fall: the stream stage only confirms what the 5 windows agree on.
  • F2 · Chute01 at 1421.9 sJF's queries start the fall at an earlier motion burst (1413.7 s) and propose a 10 s span with P up to .98. Its refiner keeps it: tIoU 0.17, a miss plus a false alarm. In JF2, four of five proposals start at 1421.9 s and only one weak one (P .37) still reaches back to 1413.7 s. The slot picks 1421.90–1423.58 s: tIoU 0.98.
  • F3 · Chute03 at 1436.9 sJF proposes only two weak, wide spans (P .38 and .28, 1433.6–1440.0 s), and its refiner gives no slot above .05: missed. JF2's queries are confident (P .98–.99, 3 windows), and its slot says P 1.000 for 1437.52–1439.60 s. That starts 0.66 s late: tIoU 0.49, counted at tIoU .1 and .3 but not .5.
  • Burst at 1450 sBetween F3 and F4, JF proposes a short burst (P .99) as its own group, and the refiner keeps it: false alarm. JF2 does not propose that burst on its own. Its only long proposal there (1437.6–1451.0 s) ends within 3 s of the F4 group and joins it. The F4 group's slots then give a single fall.
  • F4 · Chute03 at 1452.9 sBoth models find it, 0.9 s late (JF2 tIoU 0.51, JF 0.58). JF2's group has 10 members because the 3 s proximity rule pulled in the proposals around F3 as well. The region is capped at 24 s, centred on the reference span.
  • The three decoders agreeOn this stretch JF2's dense head (Viterbi) also finds all four falls, and stage 1 with the fixed merge does too. For JF, the dense head finds F1, F2 and F4, and the fixed merge finds F1 and F4 plus the 1450 s burst. The stream stage matters most where stage 1 is unsure.

Inside the refiner: the event physics of F2 and F3

For each group the refiner gets parameter-free features of the scene around the reference span [a, b]. The state of a radar at time t is d(x, before) / (d(x, before) + d(x, after)): 0 where the scene looks like the "before" profile, 1 where it looks like the "after" profile. A fall shows as a step from 0 to 1 on the radar that sees the person. The radars are mixed by softmax(β · log change), with learned β = 5.12. JF took the profiles from fixed offsets around the span (hatched). JF2 takes the stillest stretches near it (orange).

Bottom strip: mean motion over the radars (log energy of the frame-to-frame change, smoothed), from which the stillest stretches are picked. The anchor weights use the learned β of the fold D seed 0 SWA checkpoint.

6. Training on the official split

Two more runs train JF2 on the official training subjects only and validate on the 5 official val streams. x1 trains on the 13 official training streams. x8 trains on 8 stitchings of the official training clips (104 streams, about 50 h). The extra stitchings were built with the same generator as the CV, and the first two are identical to CV fold D's training streams. The streams of folds A–C were not used, because they contain the official val subjects. Every run takes the same number of optimizer steps as the CV fold D runs (2708 batches per epoch, 15 epochs), so only the variety of the training data changes. x1 and x8 have one seed each.

7. Open questions and next steps

  1. Re-run the error analysis on JF2

    Check the groups the changes were aimed at: recall on low-similarity sessions (D2 subjects 1, 2, 3, 9), falls ≤ 2.5 s after a join, the start error of falls over 2 s, and false alarms inside fall recordings. This tells whether each cause was fixed or the gain came from elsewhere.

  2. Ablate the seven changes

    Remove one group at a time (input: clutter + raw drop + bg swap; anchors; span loss; grouping + centre suppression), 2 seeds each. The ladder suggests the input and loss changes carry most of the gain.

  3. Start times

    The start error got worse (0.14–0.16 s vs 0.10 s), and F3 and F4 above start 0.7–0.9 s late. Look at whether the wider σ (0.25 s) or the IoU loss trades start accuracy for overlap, and whether a start-specific term helps.

  4. Fold D

    JF2's remaining gap to the join-based models is on the official split. Break its false positives and misses down by fall type and session there.
